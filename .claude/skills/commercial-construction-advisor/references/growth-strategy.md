# Growth strategy to $400M

Source authorities: FMI M&A reports, ESOP Association, NCEO ESOP data, public 10-Ks of construction roll-ups, ENR Top 400 historical growth analysis.

## Growth lever sequencing

- [ ] $50M → $100M: deepen 1–2 sectors, hire first PX, build precon function
- [ ] $100M → $250M: add second region or sector, install COO, formal capture process, audited financials
- [ ] $250M → $400M: regional GMs or sector verticals, CHRO, CFO upgrade, board governance, ESOP runway

## Sector diversification

- [ ] Non-correlated sector pairing (e.g., healthcare + industrial + mission critical)
- [ ] Sector-entry playbook (hire-known-leader vs. acquire vs. follow-the-customer)
- [ ] Floor of revenue per sector to justify dedicated estimating + PM bench

## Geographic expansion

- [ ] Follow-the-client vs. plant-the-flag
- [ ] Local hire vs. parachute-team trade-off
- [ ] Office overhead break-even (~$30–50M annual revenue per region)
- [ ] Licensing, sales tax, multi-state payroll prerequisites

## M&A vs. organic

- [ ] Tuck-in for capability (precon, VDC, sector entry)
- [ ] Platform acquisition for scale
- [ ] Cultural-due-diligence checklist
- [ ] Integration playbook (100-day plan)
- [ ] Earn-out structures common in construction M&A

## ESOP transitions

- [ ] When to consider (founder liquidity, tax posture, retention need)
- [ ] Partial vs. 100% S-corp ESOP
- [ ] Transaction mechanics (valuation, seller note, warrants, repurchase obligation)
- [ ] Trustee selection
- [ ] Repurchase obligation modeling
- [ ] Leverage capacity post-transaction (surety scrutiny)

## Leadership succession + governance

- [ ] CEO succession bench depth
- [ ] Outside board seats (independent + industry)
- [ ] Compensation committee discipline
- [ ] Annual strategy + capital plan rhythm

## Decision rubric: Path-to-$400M plan

- [ ] Inputs: current revenue, GP %, backlog, bonding, equity, key talent gaps
- [ ] Outputs: 5-year revenue and margin trajectory, sequenced talent hires, sector/geography moves, capital build, ESOP/M&A optionality
